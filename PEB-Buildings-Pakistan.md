## What Are Pre-Engineered Steel Buildings?

A pre-engineered steel building is a structure manufactured using engineered steel components that are designed and fabricated before being transported to the project location.

These components are assembled at the construction site using approved connections, bolts and welding procedures where specified.

Typical components include:

- Primary steel frames.
- Main columns and rafters.
- Secondary structural members.
- Roof purlins.
- Wall girts.
- Bracing systems.
- Roof and wall cladding.
- Anchor bolts and base plates.
- Structural connections.
- Mezzanine floor systems.

The design is developed according to building dimensions, imposed loads, wind conditions, applicable building codes and operational requirements.

## Main Components of a PEB Structure

### Primary Structural Members

Primary members generally consist of columns and rafters designed to support the building's principal structural loads.

They may use built-up or rolled steel sections depending on the engineering design.

### Secondary Structural Members

Secondary components include:

- Z and C purlins.
- Wall girts.
- Eave struts.
- Sag rods.
- Bridging systems.
- Structural bracing.

These members support cladding and contribute to the structural stability of the building.

### Roof and Wall Cladding

Building envelopes can incorporate:

- Galvanized steel sheets.
- Pre-painted roofing sheets.
- Insulated sandwich panels.
- Profiled metal sheets.
- Wall cladding systems.
- Roof ventilation arrangements.

Material selection depends on weather exposure, thermal requirements, building usage and project specifications.

## PEB Design and Engineering Considerations

Every industrial steel building must be designed according to its actual structural requirements.

Important considerations include:

- Building width and length.
- Eave height and ridge height.
- Clear span requirements.
- Roof slope.
- Wind loading.
- Dead loads and imposed loads.
- Crane loads where applicable.
- Seismic requirements where applicable.
- Foundation and soil conditions.
- Steel grade and material specifications.
- Connection design.
- Corrosion protection.
- Roof drainage.
- Future expansion requirements.

Engineering calculations and approved structural drawings are essential for safe construction.
